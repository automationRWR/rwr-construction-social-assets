01 · BEFORE YOU BEGIN

The shortlist is yours to win.

Reaching interview for a senior appointment is an achievement in itself. At this level, shortlists are small and every candidate is credible on paper, so the interview becomes the deciding factor.

Experienced professionals can be tempted to let their track record speak for itself. The candidates who secure senior roles treat the interview as a strategic exercise: they research in depth, articulate their impact with clarity and assess the organisation as rigorously as it assesses them.

Two-wayAt senior level, the interview is a mutual assessment. You are evaluating them as closely as they are evaluating you.

The sections that follow distil what we see from the other side of the table, so you can walk in prepared, composed and ready to lead the conversation.

02 · RESEARCH AND PREPARATION

Know the organisation.

Senior candidates are judged on how quickly they could add value, so preparation needs to go well beyond the website. Build a clear picture of the organisation's strategy, performance, leadership and challenges, and where this role fits within them.

Use what you learn to shape your answers rather than to recite it. Interviewers want to see that you understand their context and can speak credibly about the priorities you would bring to the role.

Where to start

  • Annual reports, strategy documents and recent announcements.
  • Current and pipeline projects, key clients and the sectors the business delivers in.
  • Market conditions affecting the business: procurement trends, funding cycles and competitor activity.
  • The leadership team and board, including their backgrounds and recent appointments.
  • Your recruiter's briefing on the role, the reporting line, the team and why the position is open.
TIPS
  • Map the role to the business. Identify two or three priorities you would focus on in your first six months.
  • Confirm the format in advance: who is on the panel, and whether a presentation or case study is expected.
  • Keep it professional. Hold off on LinkedIn connection requests to your interviewers until the process is complete.
KEYUnderstand the strategy, not just the services.
03 · EXECUTIVE PRESENCE

Presence speaks first.

At senior level, the way you present is read as a signal of how you lead. Your preparation, composure and judgement are on show before you answer a single question. Presence is not polish for its own sake: it is giving the panel confidence that you would represent their organisation well, with their people, their partners and their board.

How you show up

  • Be composed and unhurried. A considered pause reads as judgement, not hesitation.
  • Listen fully, then answer the question that was asked.
  • Read the room. Match the panel's style and adjust your level of detail.

What to wear

  • Dress for the role. Corporate and client-facing positions call for business attire. If a site visit is part of the process, ask what to bring.
  • Coming from site? Let the interviewer know in advance and allow time to change.
  • Keep accessories understated and grooming immaculate. The detail is noticed.
  • Prepare everything the day before: outfit, documents, directions and any presentation materials.
  • For video interviews, test your camera, lighting, sound and background, and dress exactly as you would in person.
TIPS
  • Senior networks are smaller than they appear. Your presence is part of your professional reputation.
  • Be yourself. If the culture or values aren't right for you, the interview is the place to find out.
  • Speak about your team with generosity. How you describe others says a great deal about how you lead.
GOLDEN RULE

Present as the leader they are looking to appoint.

04 · INTERVIEW DAY

Set the tone early.

Approach the interview as a conversation between peers. The panel is testing your capability, judgement and fit; you are testing the organisation's direction, culture and leadership. Bring confidence and genuine curiosity in equal measure.

Expect a structured process. Senior appointments often involve several stages, including panel interviews, presentations, psychometric assessments and meetings with executives or board members. Ask your recruiter to brief you on each one.

Getting started

  • Arrive five to ten minutes early, composed and ready. Allow extra time for parking and building security.
  • Bring printed copies of your CV, any presentation material and a short list of questions.

  • If you are presenting, rehearse to time and be ready for challenge on your assumptions.
  • Treat everyone you meet with the same respect, from reception to the chief executive. Impressions are shared.
04 · INTERVIEW DAY CONTINUED

Lead with evidence.

Senior interviews move quickly beyond your CV to how you think, lead and deliver. Expect probing follow-up questions designed to test the depth of your experience.

Be candid. If you don't know an answer, say so and explain how you would find out. Credibility matters more than having every answer.

Prepare five or six strong examples that show your leadership range: leading change, managing risk, building teams, delivering results and handling difficult stakeholders. Quantify your impact wherever you can.

Behavioural questions

These explore how you have actually led. Expect "Tell me about a time you led a team through significant change" or "Give me an example of a difficult decision made with incomplete information."

Strategic questions

These test your judgement and thinking at the level of the role, such as "How would you approach your first 90 days?" or "What would you do if a flagship project fell significantly behind programme and budget?"

Motivation questions

Questions about you and your reasons for the move, such as:

  • Why this role, and why now?
  • How would your team describe your leadership?
  • What would you do differently from your predecessor?

Use the STAR technique.

04 · INTERVIEW DAY CONTINUED

Close with impact.

A good interviewer will guide the conversation, but at senior level you are also expected to steer it with substance. Make sure the panel leaves with a clear picture of the value you would bring.

Your questions are part of the assessment. They show how you think, what you prioritise and how seriously you are weighing the move.

3–5considered questions is ideal. Ask what success looks like in the first 12 months, the challenges facing the team and how the leadership team works together.
  • Be prepared to discuss remuneration, but let the organisation raise it first. Your recruiter can manage the detail of the package on your behalf.
  • Remember that you are assessing them too. A senior move is a significant decision, so gather what you need to make the right one.

Close by summarising your interest and fit in a sentence or two, thank the panel for their time and confirm the next steps.

TIPS
  • Switch your phone off completely.
  • Keep notes brief and refer to them only when you need to.
  • Watch the time and keep answers focused.
  • Stay composed under challenge. It is often deliberate.
KEYLeave the panel clear on the value you would bring.
05 · AFTER THE INTERVIEW

Maintain momentum.

The process doesn't end when you leave the room. How you conduct yourself between stages is part of the assessment.

Reflect and follow up

Note what went well, what you would refine and any questions the conversation raised for you. A brief, personalised thank-you note within 24 hours reinforces your interest, for example:

"Thank you for your time today. I valued our discussion about [priority] and I am even more enthusiastic about the opportunity to contribute to [organisation]. I look forward to hearing about the next steps."
If you're working with a recruiter, copy them in.

Manage the process

Senior appointments often take longer, with several stages, executive or board approvals and thorough reference checks. Stay close to your recruiter, raise any concerns openly and respond promptly to requests.

TIPS
  • Brief your referees in advance on the role and the strengths it calls for. At this level, references carry significant weight.

06 · WHEN IT'S NOT A YES

Leave the door open.

At senior level, not being selected is rarely a reflection of capability. Decisions often come down to fine margins: a particular mix of experience, the dynamics of the existing team or simply timing.

Respond graciously. Thank the panel for their time and let them know you would welcome future conversations. Senior networks are small, and how you handle the outcome will be remembered.

Ask your recruiter for feedback. Constructive insight from the panel is valuable, and a recruiter can often obtain a more candid view than a direct request would.

Preferred candidates do withdraw or accept counter-offers, so remain a strong second choice. Stay connected with the people you met and keep following the organisation's direction.

KEYHow you handle the outcome shapes your reputation.
